# Source Audit

## Primary patent

Daniel Didrick, articulated artificial finger assembly, US20050043822A1 / US6908489B2.

Key mechanical facts used in the reconstruction:

- Stationary Matrix 1 attaches to the residual finger/knuckle area.
- Bracket 2 is **pivotally attached** to Matrix 1.
- Upper branch attaches through pivot 4a.
- Lower branch attaches through pivot 13a.
- Upper branch connects to Pivot Block 10 through pivot 11.
- Lower branch connects to Pivot Block 10 through pivots 13b.
- Outer Phalange 18 and Inner Phalange 26 form an adjustable phalanx assembly.
- Link 35 connects the drive to Pivot Head 33.
- Pivot Head 33 rotates around pivot 32.
- Fingertip assembly connects distally through fork 38 / lock 39 / pivot 40.

## Independent technical source

French technical sheet (Revue Technologie / Eduscol):

- fully mechanical, body-powered;
- described as a stack of two four-bar systems;
- adjustable phalanx lengths;
- 316L stainless steel;
- mechanism mass reported under 10 g, approximately 30 g with cover.

## Contemporary product / assembly evidence

Assembly and promotional videos supplied by the project owner show:

- modular hand-side interface;
- movable control clamp / hinge section;
- upper and lower adjustable perforated branches;
- matched hole-index positioning between upper and lower branches;
- documented fastener marking M1.4×5L in assembly instructions;
- physical Z-offset between linkage plates.

## Manufacturing evidence

Historical SolidWorks / reverse-engineering case studies indicate:

- many size/configuration variants rather than one universal geometry;
- SolidWorks-based digital recreation;
- EDM manufacturing;
- laser scanning used for patient fit;
- large configuration families across finger length/thickness.

## Audit conclusions

Earlier numeric candidates inferred from schematic drawings are **not accepted as manufacturing dimensions**.

The previously explored normalized candidate `AB=1, AC≈2.05, CD≈0.70, BD≈2.15` is retained only as a solver experiment.

The current master topology must include a moving input bracket relative to ground.
